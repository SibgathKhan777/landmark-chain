# Recording plan: the 60-second video

Generated by `python build_recording_plan.py` from the saved results of a real run (`out/live/results.json`). Narration time is an estimate (2.5 words a second); the call length is estimated from that run's timings and varies by a second or two each time. Rehearse once and time it.

**Plan length: 0:55.2** against a 60-second limit, so about 4.8 seconds of slack. The scripted call is about 38 seconds of that; narration is about 16 seconds, all of it before or after the call, never during it.

## The one message

> A customer speaks Hindi, a biker hears Kannada, each window only hears its own language, and the interpreter says out loud what it cannot translate.

The middle panel of the stage page shows the English meaning of every turn, so viewers who do not read Hindi or Kannada can follow.

## Before you record

1. Start it: `python live_call.py`, then open `http://127.0.0.1:8770/` in a browser. The first click on the page turns the sound on.
2. Record at **1920×1080**, with the whole stage in frame: two windows, the middle panel, and the badges at the top.
3. Keep the badges in frame: they say *synthetic voices* and the simulated noise level. Do not crop them out.
4. The bike-noise level is **5 dB** by default. See "What you will see" below: at that level one turn is refused. Quieter bike (`python live_call.py --biker-snr 10`) is a different condition, not a better take of the same one. Pick one, film it, and say which in the post.
5. Close the terminal, `.env` and anything showing your API key. Hide bookmarks and notifications.
6. Do a 10-second test recording first and play it back with sound. **Capturing the computer's own audio on a Mac usually needs extra software or a recent OBS; I have not tested it on your machine.** Without it the video has no Hindi or Kannada sound, only the captions.
7. Rehearse the whole thing once. The same script gives the same transcripts every time (Prisma returned identical text for identical audio, in every run), so rehearsing does not change what the call shows; only the timings and the spoken translations differ slightly.
8. The microphone button sends a real person's voice to Gnani's API. Do not use it in the video: the contest asks for synthetic data.
9b. **A more natural call** is available: start the app with `python live_call.py --biker-snr 10` and press *Play realistic call (AI)* (it shows only when the AI fallback is on). It is a six-turn delivery call, longer than the 60-second plan, and it **uses the AI**, so any video of it must say so. At the default 5 dB the rider's second line is misheard and the AI translates the misheard text; see the README. `python live_call.py --biker-snr 10 --replay-ai` plays the SAVED realistic call with no model running (the AI answers are replayed from a real recorded run); a video of that must say the AI answers were recorded.
9. **AI fallback:** leave it off for the video (no `LLM_BASE_URL` in `.env`). The badge at the top then reads "rule-based, no LLM" and the narration below is true. If you switch it on, the badge changes, turns it translates are labelled *AI translated*, and the narration and your post must say the AI fallback was used.

## What not to claim

Each row is something the project does not support, and why. The narration above contains none of these (a test checks the banned phrases).

| Do not say or imply | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Real-time", "instant" or "live" translation, or "on a real phone call" | It is a scripted call between synthetic voices, and the other window starts speaking about 1 to 2 seconds after the speaker stops. Nothing ran on a phone line. | "A scripted demo call" |
| "Streaming" | The translation starts when the speaker stops. Only whole utterances are processed. | Leave it out |
| "AI translation" or "it translates Hindi to Kannada" | Translation is a fixed table lookup on landmark words and seven direction clauses. There is no language model and no machine translation, unless you switch on the optional AI fallback (the badge at the top then says so). | "It translates landmarks and a few set directions" |
| "Gnani translates" | Gnani provides Prisma (speech to text) and Timbre (text to speech). The translation step is ours. | "Built on Gnani Prisma and Timbre" |
| That the Kannada is correct or natural | It was drafted without a native-speaker review. Clean-audio checks show it can be spoken and recognised, not that it sounds natural. | "The Kannada awaits native review" |
| Any accuracy number or percentage | The numbers are single samples on synthetic audio. Timbre returns different audio every time, so a re-run differs. | Show what happened; do not quote a rate |
| That it works in traffic or noise | The noise is simulated, and the saved run has a turn that failed under it. | "With simulated noise" |
| That it understands conversation | It refuses times, numbers, reasons, small talk, questions and "do not turn". | "It says so when it cannot translate" |
| That riders, delivery companies or customers use or want it | No buyer or rider has been asked. | Leave it out |
| That it solves failed deliveries | How often landmarks cause failures was not measured. | Leave it out |
| "First", "only", "best", "no AI" | Unverifiable. Prisma and Timbre are AI models; only the translation step is rule-based. | "The translation step uses fixed tables, not a language model" |
| That the voices are a real customer or biker | Every voice is a Gnani Timbre voice. | "Synthetic voices" (also in the badge at the top) |

## If something goes wrong

- **There is no sound:** click anywhere on the page once, then press Play again. Browsers block sound until you click.
- **A turn shows an error:** the Gnani API allows one request at a time and may be rate-limiting you. Wait a few seconds and press Play again. Check your credit balance.
- **The take runs over 60 seconds:** shorten the narration after the call (shot 3) first. The call itself cannot be paused.
- **A native speaker corrects the Kannada:** fix the table in `translate.py` or `directions.py`, rerun `python build_review_sheet.py`, `python live_call.py --run` and this script, and record again.
