# Landmark Chain

A voice interpreter and route memory for landmark-based addresses, built on Gnani AI's **Prisma** (speech to text) and **Timbre** (text to speech).
An entry for the Great Indian AI Internship Challenge.

**One-page solution pitch:** [PITCH.md](PITCH.md).

- **Live call interpreter:** two caller windows. A Hindi-speaking customer says "come straight, take a right, you will see a building ahead, wait there, I am coming"; the biker's window receives it only in Kannada (or English, their choice) and the reply returns in Hindi. It translates landmark phrases, a small closed set of directions and plans, and a few set phrases, and refuses out loud everything else.
- **Route memory:** customers' descriptions are merged into one route per house, and a rider confirms each landmark by voice.

**Status:** a working prototype that runs on synthetic data. It has not been tried with real customers, riders, phone lines or recordings.

## The live call

`python live_call.py` serves two phone-style caller windows on `http://127.0.0.1:8770/` (or one window each at `/phone?role=customer` and `/phone?role=biker`, for two browsers or two devices on the same network). The customer calls, the biker's window rings and shows an incoming call, and once answered each person talks into their own window. A turn runs **Prisma, then the rules in `translate.py` and `directions.py`, then Timbre**, and the result goes to the other window only:

- each window receives audio and captions in **one language**: its own (Hindi or Kannada) or English, which each person can switch to. The speaker's original voice is never sent to the other window;
- the speaker's own window shows what Prisma heard, so a mishearing is visible;
- a centre panel (an operator view that no caller hears) shows the English meaning and the time each stage took.

The scripted call plays synthetic Timbre voices through the **real pipeline**: only the speakers' clips are cached, never the translation. The hold-to-speak button sends *your* voice to Gnani's API; the contest asks for synthetic data, so use the scripted call for the video. The microphone path has been tried by one person, the developer: Hindi sentences were recognised, but one casual Kannada sentence ("where are you bro, I'm here") came back as unrelated words, even though Prisma transcribed the same sentence perfectly in four clean synthetic recordings in two voices (so it is not the vocabulary). The capture was then changed: the microphone stays open during a connected call and records 0.3 s before the press and 0.25 s after the release, and a quiet recording is brought up (never down, at most x8). **That change has not been verified on a real voice yet.** The operator panel shows each microphone turn's length and level, and `SAVE_MIC_AUDIO=1` keeps the recordings in `out/live/mic/` for diagnosing a problem (off by default, because it stores a real voice).

Directions and plans are a **closed grammar of seven clause shapes**: come or go straight, turn left or right, "you will see X ahead", wait there or here, "I am coming", "I am standing here", "I can see the building". The customer's Hindi (Devanagari or Roman script) is understood for all seven; from the biker only the last three are understood in Kannada. A sentence with a negation ("do not turn right"), a question or two opposite turns is refused, because a statement written from it would be wrong. Hindi output uses the polite plural for the biker's own statements, because the interpreter does not know the speaker's gender.

**Hinglish.** {{HINGLISH_LINE}} What that testing found, all fixed and now covered by the test:

- a partial translation used to drop words that change the meaning. "when you see the building, turn right" came out as "turn right", "the driver is coming straight" as "come straight", "should I come straight" as "come straight". Now, if the words left over are a condition (when, if, until), a third person, a distance or count (metres, lanes, signals), or a turn with no side ("u turn"), the whole sentence is refused. Harmless leftovers (filler words, "five minutes") are still announced as partial;
- "right, then left" used to be refused as a conflict; it is a valid route when "then" separates the turns, and is still refused when nothing does ("right left");
- English words in Hindi sentences, in either script (`straight`, `स्ट्रेट`, `wait karo`, `वेट`, `turn kar lo`, `right hand side`, `सीधे हाथ`, which means the *right* hand, not "straight");
- real Prisma wrote `मत` ("don't") for a spoken `mud jao` ("turn"). The interpreter refused it rather than reversing it, but it shows speech recognition can flip a meaning before the rules see it. The biker's read-back is what catches that.

### The optional AI fallback (off by default)

The rules cannot translate free description such as "the guy in the blue T-shirt will be visible". If you set `LLM_BASE_URL` (and `LLM_API_KEY`) in `.env`, the app asks an OpenAI-style model endpoint, intended to be Gnani's open-weight **Evon v3.3**, but **only** when the rules could not translate a whole Hindi/Hinglish sentence, and only for Kannada or English output. The answer is spoken only if `guard.py` finds that the details that change a route match what was said: left/right, negation, question, condition (if/when/until), colours, numbers (including digits said as words, such as "six seven nine" = 679) and, for three verbs only, who does it (standing, coming and "where": "where are you" is not "where am I"). Otherwise the rules' own result stands. With the AI on, a partial translation by the rules is spoken on its own only if the rules understood at least nine in ten of the words (`COVERAGE_MIN = 0.9` in `live_call.py`); with less, a small fragment such as "I am coming" is not spoken and the turn is refused out loud, because the unknown rest might change its meaning. With the AI off, partial translations are spoken as before. Turns it translates are labelled **AI translated** on both windows and in the operator panel, and the stage page's badge changes from "rule-based, no LLM". `LLM_GUARD=off` in `.env` turns the check off for experiments: the AI's answer is then spoken even when the check would have rejected it, the turn is labelled **AI translated, NOT checked**, and the operator panel still shows what the check would have said. Every measurement and recorded result in this README is with the check ON. It also works the other way: when the biker's Kannada (as Prisma wrote it) is something the rules cannot translate, the AI writes Hindi (or English) for the customer, with the same check, which reads the Kannada source. A Kannada source is whatever Prisma heard, so a misheard word is translated faithfully: Prisma once wrote `ಎಲ್ಲದ ಬ್ರೋ ನಾನು ಇಲ್ಲದ` for a casual "where are you bro, I'm here" (a real microphone recording with a bad capture), and the model invented fluent sentences from it; the check rejected them only because the invented ones contained a "not" the source lacked.

Gnani has no hosted language-model API (its documented API is speech to text, text to speech and voice cloning), so Evon has to be self-hosted. `kaggle/serve_on_kaggle.sh` starts it on Kaggle's free two-T4 GPUs with the community 4/5-bit file, behind an API key and a temporary public address, for up to 3 hours. Check Kaggle's terms for tunnels first; the address changes every run.

## The interpreter's tables

The customer says, in Hindi, "भैया ये गेट दूसरा गेट दायीं तरफ वाला" (brother, this gate... the second gate, the one on the right). The biker hears, in Kannada, `ಬಲಗಡೆಯ ಎರಡನೇ ಗೇಟ್`. The biker's read-back goes back to the customer in Hindi.

Prisma turns each speaker's audio into text, the extractor reduces it to language-neutral slots, and the slots are written out in the other language from fixed tables, then spoken by Timbre. By default there is **no language model and no machine translation** (an optional AI fallback exists and is described below; it is off in every saved result), so it can only say what the tables can say: landmark phrases built from {{VOCAB_LINE}}, plus {{PHRASEBOOK_N}} set phrases (yes, no, found it, could not find it, please repeat). English is produced for on-screen meaning only and is never spoken.

## The route memory and rider confirmation

Addresses are often given as landmarks: "behind the temple, the blue gate" (we did not measure how common that is). Landmark Chain turns such descriptions into a route a rider can follow by voice.

1. **Customers** describe the house by voice, in Hindi or Marathi. Prisma transcribes. A rule-based extractor reduces each phrase to language-neutral slots: anchor (temple, school, tank...), relation (behind, in front of, after...), colour, ordinal (second lane) and side.
2. **A SQLite store** merges the descriptions of several customers, per pincode and locality, into one route. Confidence rises with evidence and with a rider's confirmation, falls on an explicit "not found", and fades as evidence ages. A Hindi and a Marathi description of the same house land on the same entry, because the slots are language-neutral.
3. **The rider** hears the route in their own language (Timbre) and repeats each landmark. A verifier compares the transcript with the expected landmark word by word and decides:

| Decision | Meaning | What the bot does |
|---|---|---|
| ACCEPT | every critical word of the expected landmark was heard, and nothing contradicts it | confirms and moves on |
| CONFIRM | nothing contradicts it, but some words are missing, or the transcript contains a negation | reads the landmark back and asks yes or no |
| MISMATCH | a word was heard with a different value (another colour, lane or side), or two values were heard at once | asks again, then falls back to a yes/no question |
| ASK_AGAIN | empty, too short, or nothing matched | asks again, and never picks the nearest candidate |

After three failed attempts the rider is offered a button. A voice failure never counts against a landmark; only an explicit "no" or a button press does.

## What is real and what is simulated

| Part | Status |
|---|---|
| Speech recognition | Real Gnani Prisma v2.5 output |
| All voices (customers, rider, bot) | Gnani Timbre v2.5 synthetic voices. No human recordings |
| Noise | Simulated: white noise, and a "bike" mix of synthetic engine hum plus babble made from other Timbre clips |
| Customers, riders, addresses, pincode | Invented. No personal or real customer data |
| The rider's button press, and the "yes" in the replay scene | Simulated, and labelled wherever they appear |

## Quick start

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
cp .env.example .env              # then put your Gnani API key in .env. Never commit it
python smoke_test.py              # checks Prisma and Timbre work with your key (about 20 API calls)
python live_call.py               # the live call: open http://127.0.0.1:8770/ and press "Play scripted call"
python live_call.py --run         # the same scripted call without a browser; saves out/live/results.json (about ten API calls)
python live_call.py --run --biker-snr 10   # a quieter bike, saved separately as results_bike10.json
python call_demo.py               # the older recorded replay of a call; responses are cached in out/call/cache, so repeats are free
python build_call_page.py         # writes out/call/index.html (a static replay page). Open it in a browser
python demo.py --stress --replay  # the route-memory demo; cached in out/demo/cache
python build_page.py              # writes out/demo/index.html
python -m unittest discover -s tests   # offline tests, no API calls
```

The generated replay pages (`out/demo`, `out/call`) work from a double-click (`file://`), need no server and make no network requests. The live call needs `python live_call.py` running and the internet.

## Findings that shaped the design

**Background speech hurts more than engine noise.** {{NOISE_COMPARE}}

**A two-word utterance was unreliable, so the rider says a full sentence.** One landmark spoken three ways (`experiments/short_audio.py`: one Timbre voice, one synthesis of each, the same simulated noise, so a small sample):

{{SHORT_TABLE}}

The demo therefore has the rider say "I see the blue gate" and not just "blue gate". {{YESNO_LINE}} An earlier, unsaved check had returned an empty transcript for one-word Marathi replies; the saved experiment did not reproduce that, so the silence padding in `gnani.py` is a precaution, not a measured need.

**Results vary between runs because Timbre does.** {{DETERMINISM_LINE}} Noise seeds are fixed, but the synthesised speech is not, so a re-run generates different clips. The demo cache and the saved `out/sweep_*.wav` clips make those runs repeatable.

**The extractor is the weakest part.** It is a closed word list. Recall on phrases written after the last round of fixes (set C) is the honest estimate; sets A and B were used to extend the list, so their numbers are inflated.

{{EXTRACTOR_TABLE}}

What it misses are landmark nouns it has never seen (a pan shop, a church, a station), and it can mistake a person's name for a colour ("हरी भाई" read as green). A closed list cannot cover landmark names, which are an open set.

## Known limitations

- Extraction is rule-based and limited to the word lists; unseen landmark nouns are missed. A description with no recognised word is dropped without feedback to the customer.
- Hindi, Marathi (route memory) and Kannada (call interpreter), plus Roman-script Hinglish for the words in the list. Other languages are not covered.
- The interpreter translates only landmark phrases, the seven direction and plan clauses and {{PHRASEBOOK_N}} set phrases. Numbers, times, names, directions that start from a landmark ("from the bus stand turn right"), conditions ("when you see..."), distances ("after two lanes"), statements about someone else, questions and "not the blue gate" are refused. Kannada "ಸರಿ" is always written as "yes, that is right", even when it only means "OK". A misheard word is translated faithfully, so the biker's read-back is what catches it.
- The Kannada wording has not been reviewed by a native speaker.
- The call is scripted with synthetic voices, not a live phone call; it works at utterance level (not streaming), one Gnani request at a time, and "ready after" is the time it takes after the speaker stops, which is 1 to 2 s on this machine, not instant. Under bike noise Prisma can cut a word short (it wrote `ಕಾಣಿಸಿ` for `ಕಾಣಿಸಿತು`), and the interpreter then refuses the turn.
- Prisma returns no confidence score, so "unsure" is inferred from mismatch with the expected landmark.
- A spoken "yes" to a read-back can be a reflex answer. A rider who answers "no" ends the checkpoint as "not found", and nothing yet reroutes them.
- Everything is synthetic; real accents, handsets, traffic and background speech are untested. The demo is a prototype with a generated page, not a mobile app.
- No buyer has been asked whether they would pay for this. Any business case is a hypothesis.
- A rider must use voice hands-free. Phone use while riding is a safety and legal question this project does not address.

## Privacy and secrets

- The API key lives only in `.env`, which is git-ignored, as is `out/`. `.env.example` holds a placeholder. This folder is not a git repository yet; keep `.env` ignored if you create one.
- The store hashes customer ids with a random per-database salt, replaces spoken digits with `#`, keeps only the recognised landmark words and never stores audio. `forget_customer` erases a person's evidence. This is data minimisation, not a compliance review.
